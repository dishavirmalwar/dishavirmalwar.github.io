--- layout: single title: "Research" permalink: /research/ author_profile: true --- My research focuses on atmospheric dynamics, particularly the processes governing large-scale circulation, tropical–extratropical interactions, and extreme weather events.

Rossby Wave Breaking

Rossby Wave Breaking (RWB) involves the irreversible overturning of potential vorticity contours near the dynamical tropopause and plays an important role in large-scale atmospheric circulation and the exchange of air between the tropics and extratropics. While RWB is commonly observed during boreal winter, its characteristics and role during the summer monsoon remain less understood. My research focuses on a comprehensive analysis of RWB during the summer monsoon—evaluating its spatial distribution, frequency, latitudinal extent, and long-term trends, as well as its relationship with monsoon variability. Using potential vorticity data on the 350 K isentropic surface and the WaveBreaking package, I analyse ERA5 reanalysis data for 1980–2019.

Atmospheric Blocking and Extreme Events

Atmospheric blocking consists of persistent, quasi-stationary high-pressure systems that disrupt normal westerly flow, trapping weather patterns and generating severe, prolonged anomalies such as heatwaves and extreme rainfall. By anchoring stationary wave packets, blocking highs can excite far-reaching teleconnection chains across hemisphere-scale. Current understanding is limited regarding the multi-stage conversion efficiency between upstream Atlantic blocking centers and downstream continental nodes, as well as how background flow changes alter these precursor pathways. I investigate the multi-step precursor pathway linking North Atlantic Blocking Highs (NABH) to the Central Asian High (CAH) and downstream wave breaking, applying spatial tracking tools (ConTrack) to evaluate pathway activation and precursor lead times for EM-DAT extreme monsoon events.
